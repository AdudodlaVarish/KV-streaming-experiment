# Comparator and claim boundaries

[The SLAI paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.01002) targets TBT-aware scheduling and its authors release an implementation. We inspected [agrimUT/SLAI](https://github.com/agrimUT/SLAI) at commit `5098a7aba05e3edbcfa3a509d6cc9cd248fc4380`. The repository uses the Sarathi serving engine, Python3.10, CUDA12.1 and an RTX ADA6000. Our existing GPU/runtime/model stack differs. We did not install or benchmark that original engine.

The `slai_port` comparison implements its central batch ordering in the same pinned vLLM0.30.0 allocator as the other policies: deadline-critical decodes, active prefills, shortest-prompt fresh admission, then noncritical decodes; it also follows the preference to evict an unscheduled prefill, otherwise the blocked decode itself. The generated native body keeps vLLM's asynchronous output bookkeeping. [DESIGN.md](DESIGN.md) records the concrete deviations, including batch-time estimation from dispatch-to-engine-output delay and native resumed-context handling. Results compare these policy rules in this engine; they are not a reproduction of SLAI's published numbers or a claim to outperform the complete original system. The original scheduler and README are preserved under `references/SLAI`.

[CacheOPT](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.13773v1) already combines predictive KV reservation, proactive allocation, deadline-aware resumption and victim selection. Our rolling growth allowance and corrective handoff are simpler empirical rules, with no output-length predictor. We do not claim those concepts as new. The useful question is whether their interaction addresses the physical-capacity mechanism observed in our traces and what throughput/replay cost it creates.

[Chronos](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/computer-science/articles/10.3389/fcomp.2026.1873627/full) and [its public simulator](https://github.com/am-research/rtss-ttft-tbt) provide admission/schedulability analysis for TTFT/TBT constraints. They are relevant to hard guarantees, but a CPU simulator without our paged-cache recomputation behavior is not an interchangeable GPU baseline. We do not inherit its guarantees.

Native vLLM serves running requests before new admission, so its baseline already provides considerable decode preference. The new policy's testable addition is a per-resident allowance for future KV growth plus early service correction for physically blocked started streams. Ablating the correction against `growth_only` is necessary before attributing success to a more complex scheduler.

These are research prototypes on one laptop GPU. A publication comparison would require original-system validation or independently reviewed ports, tracing-overhead measurements, larger models/contexts, natural stopping, more workload seeds and comparable target calibration.
